# Sprint 3, Learning Goal 2 - Turning the traffic lights into a proper part of the city tile with hidden wiring and safe internal cabling

## Learning Question

How can I turn my traffic-light project into four proper traffic-light models that are placed in the city tile, with the LEDs built into the models, the wires hidden under the tile, and the wiring arranged safely so the lights work clearly and do not interfere with each other?

## S — Situation

At the start of this learning goal, I already have a working traffic-light project. I have the controller, the traffic lights, and the basic switching logic working. The traffic lights can already switch correctly and the project has already been tested as a working prototype.

What is still missing is that the project is not yet built in a practical and finished way. The traffic lights are still part of a temporary setup instead of being proper parts of the city tile itself. The wiring is also still something that mainly belongs to the prototype stage, not to a neat final setup.

In practice, that is a problem. A traffic-light system for a city tile should not only work, but should also be built in a way that fits the scene and keeps the wiring organized. When wires stay visible, run loosely, or are not separated properly inside the traffic-light model, the result becomes messy and harder to manage. It can also make the traffic lights less reliable.

In this step, I want to improve that by making four 3D traffic-light models that can contain the LEDs and be placed on the city tile. I also want to make holes so the wires can go underneath the tile and stay out of sight. Inside the traffic-light models, I want to arrange and isolate the wiring so the LED connections do not touch or interfere with each other. That is important because it turns the project from a loose prototype into a cleaner and more practical traffic-light system that fits the city tile properly.

## T — Task

My task in this learning goal is to extend the current traffic-light project so the traffic lights become proper built-in parts of the city tile.

The main goal is not just to make the project look better, but to solve a practical problem. Right now, the traffic lights work, but the physical setup is still temporary and exposed. I want to change that.

To do this, I will first determine what the traffic-light models need so they can hold the LEDs properly and fit on the tile. After that, I will work out how the wires can be guided through holes in the tile so they can be hidden underneath. Then I will decide how the wiring inside the traffic-light models should be arranged and isolated so the LED connections stay separate and do not interfere with each other. After that, I will build and test the setup to check whether the four traffic lights fit well on the tile, whether the wiring can be hidden neatly, and whether the LEDs still work reliably after everything is placed into the models.

The result I want is a working setup in which four traffic lights are properly placed on the city tile, the wiring is guided neatly underneath the tile, and the LED wiring inside the traffic-light models is separated safely so the full setup is clear, stable, and practical to use.
